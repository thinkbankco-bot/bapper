04 / 09 Operations review

Where service businesses lose revenue.

Five operational gaps account for most of the lost revenue in a small service business: unanswered calls, after-hours paperwork, quotes with no follow-up, past customers with no contact, and problems found too late.

Five operational gaps we close. Select one to view the system

“The phone rings while I’m on a job. By the time I call back, they’ve booked someone else.”

Missed-call text-back

Any unanswered call receives a text within 60 seconds, under your business name, asking what the caller needs and where. The reply reaches your phone as a normal text with name, address, and problem captured.

Lands in
A text to the caller and a text to you
What it’s worth
Assuming six unanswered calls a week and one in four recovered, that is roughly 78 jobs a year at your average ticket. Assumed inputs, replaced with your own call log in the assessment.

“Every job comes with an hour of typing after it, usually at the kitchen table.”

What changes

The same Tuesday, twice.

Tuesday, without the system

  1. 2:14pmA homeowner’s furnace fails. She finds your listing and calls. You are in a crawlspace in Kent City.
  2. 2:14pmFour rings. Voicemail. She does not leave a message.
  3. 2:15pmShe calls the next company on the list.
  4. 4:50pmYou finish the job, see the missed call, and call back.

You never knew that job existed.

  2. 2:15pmShe receives a text under your business name: sorry we missed your call. What is the issue, and what is the address?
  3. 2:16pmShe replies with the problem and her street.
  4. 2:16pmThe reply reaches your phone as a normal text, address included.
  5. 4:50pmYou finish the job already knowing who to call first.

Same job site. Same phone. A different outcome.

09 / 09 Questions

What we are asked first.

Why not use the missed-call feature in the software I already pay for?

If ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, FieldEdge, or Jobber already does this and it is switched on, you do not need us. We confirm that on the first call at no charge. Usually the feature sits in a tier you do not have, sends one generic line, or was never enabled.

I already pay for an answering service. Is this the same thing?

No. An answering service takes messages from callers who wait on hold. This system reaches the callers who do not, and it follows up on open quotes. If your service is working, keep it. This sits in front of it.

I sell online. Shopify and Klaviyo already do some of this.

If your abandoned-cart flow is recovering orders, keep it. We work on what sits between the applications: a supplier invoice that does not match the purchase order, an integration that failed unnoticed, or a late-evening product question that needs your real prices and stock.

What happens in the free assessment?

Ninety minutes by phone or on site. We review where the hours go, which calls and quotes are being dropped, and how the business appears when a customer searches or asks an AI assistant. You keep the one-page summary either way.

Who owns the number, and who is responsible for compliance?

The number is registered to your business and portable to your own account at any time. Every message carries your business name and a STOP option, as carrier rules require. You approve the wording before launch and can shut the system off from your phone in seconds.

What if the system does not perform?

Half at signature, half at thirty days from go-live, due only if the system meets the target written into the proposal and measured on your own call log. If it falls short, the balance is waived, the system is shut off, and you keep the number and the templates.

Do you offer marketing services?

Yes: paid advertising, search rankings, and AI answer visibility, each run as a measured system with pass and fail criteria agreed before any spend. See Marketing & performance.

Where are you located?

Sparta, Michigan, north of Grand Rapids, serving West Michigan. Meeting in person before an engagement begins is standard practice.
