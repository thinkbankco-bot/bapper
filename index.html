OPERATIONS REVIEW

Where service businesses lose revenue.

Five operational gaps account for most of the lost revenue in a small service business: unanswered calls, after-hours paperwork, quotes with no follow-up, past customers with no contact, and problems found too late.

Five operational gaps we close. Select one to view the system

“The phone rings while I’m on a job. By the time I call back, they’ve booked someone else.”

Missed-call text-back

Any unanswered call receives a text within 60 seconds, under your business name, asking what the caller needs and where. The reply reaches your phone as a normal text with name, address, and problem captured.

Lands in
A text to the caller and a text to you
What it’s worth
At six unanswered calls a week, recovering one in four adds roughly 78 jobs a year at your average ticket.

Quotes drafted from the job itself

Photos, notes, and measurements from the site come back as a written quote, priced from your own price list in the format you already send. You review, correct, and send.

Lands in
Your inbox, in your existing quote format
What it’s worth

Automatic quote follow-up

Every open quote receives a short follow-up at two days and again the following week, in wording you approve. The sequence stops the moment the customer replies or books, and you are notified.

Lands in

We segment your existing customer records by service and date, then send a short individual note referencing their equipment and last visit. No mass blast, no discount. Records never leave your system.

QUESTIONS

Questions we are asked first.

Why not use the missed-call feature in the software I already pay for?

If ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, FieldEdge, or Jobber already does this, and it is switched on and working, you do not need us; we confirm that on the first call at no charge. Usually the feature sits in a tier you do not have, sends one generic line, or was never enabled.

I already pay for an answering service. Is this the same thing?

No, and it does not replace one. An answering service takes messages from callers who wait on hold. This system reaches the callers who do not, and it follows up on open quotes, which no answering service does. If your service is working, keep it. This sits in front of it.

I sell online. Shopify and Klaviyo already do some of this.

If your abandoned-cart flow is on and recovering orders, keep it. We work on what sits between the applications: supplier invoices that do not match the purchase order, an integration that failed unnoticed, or a 9 p.m. product question that needs your real prices and stock. If a $30-a-month application solves it, we say which one.

What happens in the free assessment?

Ninety minutes by phone or on site. We review where the hours go, which calls and quotes are being dropped, and how your business appears when a customer searches or asks an AI assistant. You receive a one-page summary naming the highest-value system to build first, with a fixed price beside it, whether or not you proceed.

Who owns the number, and who is responsible for compliance?

The number is registered to your business and portable to your own account at any time. Every message carries your business name and a STOP option, as carrier rules require. You approve all wording before launch, can review every message sent, and can shut the system off from your phone in seconds.

What if the system does not perform?

Half at signature, half at thirty days from go-live, due only if the system meets the target written into the proposal, measured on your own call log. If it falls short, the balance is waived and you keep the number, the templates, and the assessment summary.

Do you offer marketing services?

Yes: paid advertising, Google rankings, and AI search visibility, each run as a measured system with defined pass/fail criteria under the same engagement model. See Marketing systems.

Where are you located?

Sparta, Michigan, north of Grand Rapids, serving West Michigan. Meeting in person before an engagement begins is standard practice.
