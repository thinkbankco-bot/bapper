04 / 04 Who it’s for

If this is your week.

Three situations we are called about most. If none of them are yours, the assessment is still free.

01

The same customer address gets typed into three systems before the truck leaves the yard.

Nobody trusts any of the three, so the office keeps a fourth copy in a spreadsheet. You need one place that feeds the others.

02

The totals never match and the difference is always a handful of jobs that were closed in one place and not the other.

Questions

What we are asked first.

Do I have to change the software we use?

No, and we will argue against it. Replacing field software costs a season of retraining. We build against what you already pay for, and we say plainly when a setting in that software does the job and no build is needed.

What if the software has no API?

Then we tell you that before the proposal, not after. Some systems only allow scheduled exports, which is workable but slower. Some allow nothing, and in that case the honest answer is that the integration is not possible at a sane price.

Who owns the code?

You do, for anything built for you. It is delivered as readable source with a written description of what it does and where it runs, so another engineer can pick it up without calling us.

What happens when one of the systems changes an API?

That is what the monthly service plan covers: we monitor the connections, fix them when a vendor changes something, and tell you in the weekly summary. Without a plan, it becomes a fixed-scope repair.
