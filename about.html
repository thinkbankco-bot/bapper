The premise

The bottleneck is never the job. It is the work around the work.

Owners of small service businesses are excellent at the trade and self-taught at everything else. The craft is not what slows them down. What slows them down is the hour of typing after every job, the five subscriptions that do not talk to each other, the phone that rings while the crew is under somebody’s furnace, and the quote that went out in March and was never chased. None of it is the work. All of it eats the evenings.

Bapper exists for that layer. We find the one place the business actually stops, build the system that removes it, and prove it on the owner’s own records before the last payment is due. We keep the list of capabilities short and the scope of each engagement fixed, because a studio of one can either be broad or be accountable, and we chose accountable.

Positions

What we hold to.

Five positions that decide how an engagement runs.

  1. Find the bottleneck before building anything.

    The assessment comes first and it is free, because scoping a build before understanding the week is how a business ends up paying for software it does not need. The one-page summary is the customer’s whether or not anything follows.

  2. Working software beats a slide.

    Everything on the Work page runs. We would rather show a build that does something narrow and real than a diagram of a system nobody has switched on.

  3. It has to land where you already work.

    Every system we install delivers to a channel already in use: a text to the phone, the inbox already open, the spreadsheet already paid for. No new application, no new login, no new routine for the crew.

  4. Write down what “working” means before starting.

    Every proposal carries the target the system has to hit and the date it is measured on, read off the customer’s own records. That sentence is what makes the final payment arguable on evidence rather than opinion.

  5. Sometimes the right answer is no system at all.

    If a setting in software already being paid for does the job, we say so on the first call and there is no invoice. A one-person studio can afford to turn work down. It cannot afford to be wrong about fit.

Assessment, proposal, installation, plan.

Four stages, every time. No retainers, no open-ended hourly work, and no final payment until the system is measured on your own data.

  • Assessment. Ninety minutes by phone or on site. We review where the hours go, which calls and quotes are being dropped, and how the business appears when a customer searches or asks an assistant. You receive a one-page summary naming the highest-value system to build first. Free, and yours whether or not you proceed.
  • Written proposal. Fixed scope, fixed price, the target the system must hit, and the date it will be measured on. One document, no rate card, no hourly estimate.
  • Fixed-scope installation. We build it, you approve every message before launch, and the system goes live with a person watching the first week. Half at signature, half at thirty days from go-live, due only if the written target is met on your own call log.
  • Monthly service plan. Monitoring, backups, security, changes on request, and a written summary every week. Month to month. No long-term contract.

Who you will work with

The founder.

Jason Carr · Founder and lead engineer · Sparta, Michigan

Bapper is led by Jason Carr, founder and lead engineer, based in Sparta, Michigan. He spent years building automation for his own operations: document pipelines that read and generate paperwork, telephony that records and files itself, monitoring agents, and reporting tools that ran unattended overnight and left a summary by morning. The same bottlenecks stall a five-truck service company, with more riding on each one.

A one-person studio means no handoff between the person who scopes the work and the person who builds it. The number on this page reaches him. It also means a direct answer on fit: if a problem is too large for us, or solvable with a setting in software you already own, we say so on the first call rather than three weeks into a proposal.

He is not a salesperson and does not employ one. Every engagement starts with a free assessment and a one-page summary. Sometimes the honest recommendation is to change nothing, and we say so.

Where we are

Sparta, Michigan.

The studio is in Sparta, north of Grand Rapids. We serve West Michigan: Kent, Ottawa, Muskegon, Montcalm, and Newaygo counties, and the towns between them. Meeting in person before an engagement begins is standard practice, and for installations on site it is not optional.

Work outside the area is possible when the system is fully remote, but we will say plainly that a business two hours away is better served by someone who can be in the shop by lunch.
